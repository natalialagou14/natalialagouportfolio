Ingredient
Availability

The espresso beans ran out at 11am. One switch has to take them off sixty-nine dishes before the next order lands.

Classic Blend Ενεργό σε 53 προϊόντα 15 screens 3 availability states Mobile + desktop

Objective

Let a shop take an ingredient off the whole menu in one action, and know exactly how much of the menu it just changed. Availability in the BOX catalogue used to be a property of a product: to mark brown sugar as out you opened each of the eight products that use it and switched it off eight times. The ingredient itself was not a thing you could act on.

This redesign promotes the ingredient — επιλογή προϊόντος (product option) — to a row of its own, with one switch that reaches every product carrying it. The whole design problem is what that row has to tell you before you flip it, and what it does when the answer isn't a clean yes or no.

Why this screen carries the weight

  • Stock runs out mid-shift, not between shifts. The person doing this is in an apron behind a counter, and every minute the catalogue is wrong is a minute customers keep ordering something the kitchen cannot make.
  • The cost of the old way scaled with the popular ingredients. In the catalogue captured here, Decaf Water Processed sits in 69 products — sixty-nine screens to visit, with no way to tell whether you'd got them all.
  • A switch here is not a preference, it is a promise to every customer browsing the shop right now. Turning one on wrongly sells a dish that cannot be made; turning one off wrongly hides dishes that can.
  • This closes a loop the order screen only opens. Μη Διαθέσιμο Προϊόν (Unavailable item) is one of the eight reasons a shop can reject an order. Every rejection for that reason is a catalogue that was out of date before the order arrived.

Context

BOX is a Greek food delivery platform with two apps: one for customers, one for shops. This project sits in the shop-side app, under the second tab — Κατάλογος (Catalogue) — which runs on a phone in the kitchen and as a desktop app in the shop. Both are production Greek UI.

An option in BOX is anything a product is built from or served with: an ingredient (Cranberries), a preparation (Decaf Water Processed), a size (6αδα μπολ), a sauce, a sugar. One option belongs to many products; one product carries many options. Availability has to be editable from either end of that relationship, which is the reason this screen exists at all.

Four tabs, one verb

The catalogue divides into four tabs. They list four different kinds of thing, but every one of them ends in the same control on the right edge of the row: a switch that says whether customers can order it right now. The catalogue is not an editor — it is an availability board.

Products and offers are lists of things you sell. Options are the layer underneath them — and the only tab where one row changes many rows in another tab.

An ingredient has three states, not two

A product is available or it isn't. An ingredient spread across twelve products can be available in nine of them — the state the switch cannot express. Everything below follows from admitting that third state instead of rounding it to on or off.

Ενεργό · Active

On everywhere

Live in every product that carries it. The row reads Ενεργό σε 53 προϊόντα (active in 53 products) and the switch is orange.

One switch turns 53 products off
Ανενεργό · Inactive

Off everywhere

Suppressed in every product. The row greys, the count stays — Ανενεργό σε 8 προϊόντα (inactive in 8 products) — because the number is how you know what comes back.

One switch brings 8 products back
Μικτό · Mixed

On in some, off in others

The row states both halves — Ενεργό σε 4 προϊόντα / Ανενεργό σε 1 προϊόντα (active in 4, inactive in 1) — and drops its master switch entirely.

No switch. Only the chevron

That last card is the whole design in one rule: where a bulk action would have to guess, there is no bulk control. The chevron opens the per-product list, and the merchant decides five times instead of once.

Principles the screens had to hold

  • No switch without its number. Every row states how many products it reaches, in the same place, before you touch it.
  • The state sentence is the label. Ενεργό σε 14 προϊόντα (active in 14 products) is not a status badge bolted onto a name — it is the row's only description.
  • A bulk action is offered only where it is unambiguous. Mixed rows get no master switch.
  • Bulk is never blind: the mass buttons live at the bottom of the list they affect, never on the row, so you have read the products before you can act on all of them.
  • An action that is already done is not offered. Ενεργοποίηση όλων (enable all) greys out when everything is already on.
  • Destructive and restorative actions are different colours, always in the same order.
  • Every product in the breakdown carries its category in brackets — Λουκουμάς (Γλυκίσματα) — because two products can share a name and the shop has to tell them apart.
  • The counts are filters, not decoration: the three tabs above the list are the same numbers, made clickable.

The flow

01

The catalogue you arrive in

Products first, because that is what a shop thinks it sells. Each row is a name, a price and a switch; the search field above accepts both — Αναζήτησε προϊόν ή υλικό… (search a product or an ingredient) — which is the first hint that ingredients are addressable things here.

The two surfaces navigate categories differently. The phone puts them in a scrolling chip strip with a jump menu on the left; the desktop app puts them in a fixed rail and adds two more controls to every product row.

02

The ingredient list

  1. The name is on the left, the state on the right. Arabica Blend — Ενεργό σε 14 προϊόντα (active in 14 products). Subject, state, scope, in one line.

  2. The chevron opens a first view of where that ingredient is available, so the merchant can scan the situation before changing anything.

  3. Off rows tint the whole row, not just the switch, so a long list of live options reads as a list with a few holes in it.

  4. The chevron and the switch stay separate. One inspects, one acts — inspecting an option that sits in dozens of products must not risk changing it.

03

Opening one ingredient

The chevron opens Διαθεσιμότητα επιλογών (Ingredients availability) — the same ingredient, listed as the products it appears in. The list is grouped, not sorted: a heading Ενεργό σε (active in) over the products where it is live, a heading Ανενεργό σε (inactive in) over the ones where it is not. Flip a product and it moves between the groups, so the screen always reads as a current answer rather than a form you have half filled in.

04

Two buttons that know what is left to do

The footer is the same pair in every state — Ενεργοποίηση όλων (enable all) on the left, Απενεργοποίηση όλων (disable all) on the right, never reordered — but only the ones that would change something are live. Colour carries the direction: green adds availability, red removes it, grey means there is nothing to do.

05

The list you actually open at 7am

The filter that matters is the small one. Ανενεργές επιλογές (inactive options) is the shop's whole out-of-stock list on one screen. Opening the day is checking that list and turning back on whatever came in overnight.

The large filter is the reassurance. Ενεργές επιλογές (X) (active options) states how many ingredients are currently enabled, so a merchant can confirm the rest of the menu is intact without working through endless rows — the whole feature is aimed at fewer taps and less of the fatigue that comes with them.

Why bulk lives at the bottom of a list

The obvious place for Απενεργοποίηση όλων (disable all) is the row itself — a long-press, a menu, something that saves the extra tap. It is deliberately not there.

Putting the mass action inside the sheet means you cannot reach it without having been shown the products it applies to, in full, listed by name and category. Sixty-nine products is an abstraction in a list row; it is a scroll in the sheet. The extra tap is the confirmation step, which is why there is no separate confirmation dialog.

It also means bulk and individual editing share one screen. A merchant who opens the sheet intending to disable everything and then notices one product that should stay can simply not press the button and flip four switches instead. Those are not two different flows.

Phone versus desktop

Nothing structural changes between the two, and the same five steps exist on both. What changes is what the extra width is spent on.

  • The breakdown changes container. On the phone it takes the full screen, because a small device needs the whole width to list products legibly; on desktop the same content is a modal over the list.
  • Product rows gain controls. The desktop product rows carry scheduled availability and the auto-reactivation setting alongside the switch; the phone keeps price and switch only.
  • The mass actions do not move. Bottom of the sheet on both, same order, same colours — the one component that is deliberately identical, because it is the only destructive one.

Biggest challenge

Making a many-to-many relationship look like a list. An ingredient has no single availability — it has one per product it appears in — and a list row has one line and one control. Everything hard about this screen is the compression: which of the two numbers leads, what the switch does when the two disagree, what the filters do with a row that belongs in both.

The answer was to let the row be a sentence and let the switch disappear when the sentence has two halves. That is an unusual thing to ship — a control that is absent on some rows reads as a bug until you understand it — and it only holds up because the chevron beside it is always there, so the row is never a dead end.

Expected impact

  • Fewer rejections for Μη Διαθέσιμο Προϊόν (Unavailable item), because the catalogue can be corrected in the time it takes to notice the shortage.
  • Far less work per correction — one action instead of dozens, with no chance of missing a product.
  • A reliable open and close routine: the Ανενεργές (inactive) filter is a short, complete list of everything the shop is currently not serving.
  • Fewer part-cancelled orders and refunds, which cost the shop its rating as well as the order.
